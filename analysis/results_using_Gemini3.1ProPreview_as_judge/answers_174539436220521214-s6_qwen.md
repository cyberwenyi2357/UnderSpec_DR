> How does methotrexate work to modulate immune and inflammatory pathways in the treatment of moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis?

Summary of how methotrexate modulates immune and inflammatory pathways in moderate‑to‑severe atopic dermatitis (AD)

Methotrexate (MTX) is an antifolate immuno‑modulator used systemically for severe AD with extensive pruritus and refractory dermatitis. Its therapeutic effects arise from a coordinated antiproliferative, anti‑inflammatory, and immunosuppressive mechanism that corrects both the hyperproliferative epidermis and the T‑cell–driven type‑2/neutrophilic inflammation characteristic of AD. <cite id="12dc1bd3-1">MTX has a triple-action mechanism: anti-inflammatory, anti-proliferative and immunosuppressive, operating by competitively inhibiting folinic acid metabolism</cite> <cite id="12dc1bd3-7">Its immunosuppressive action reduces the intense itching, redness, and inflammation associated with eczema flares</cite>. <cite id="12dc1bd3-5">Mechanistically, MTX’s drug action is based on inhibition of dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR), blocking reduction of dihydrofolate to tetrahydrofolate required for nucleic acid synthesis</cite> <cite id="12dc1bd3-7">MTX irreversibly binds DHFR and prevents reduced folate formation, limiting DNA synthesis and mitosis</cite>.

Cell‑intrinsic biochemistry (folate pathway blockade) and broad immune modulation

Pharmacologic DHFR inhibition by MTX depletes reduced folates within rapidly proliferating immune and epithelial cells, thereby suppressing DNA synthesis, cell division, and differentiation-dependent cytokine production. <cite id="12dc1bd3-1">MTX stops the activity of folinic acid by competitive inhibition of DHFR</cite> <cite id="12dc1bd3-5">Inhibition of DHFR prevents dihydrofolate reduction to the active folate form, underpinning MTX’s mechanism</cite>. This dual action on lymphocytes and keratinocytes explains both the immunosuppressive and antiproliferative effects in AD. <cite id="12dc1bd3-9">MTX is an immunosuppressant medicine that dampens immune-system cell activity to reduce skin inflammation in eczema</cite>.

Systemically, MTX lowers pro‑inflammatory cytokines produced by T cells and macrophages. <cite id="12dc1bd3-8">Clinical observations suggest an anti‑inflammatory mechanism of action rather than a delayed immunosuppressive effect, with relatively rapid onset of clinical improvement</cite> <cite id="12dc1bd3-8">Low-dose methotrexate decreases inflammation</cite>. <cite id="12dc1bd3-5">By inhibiting DHFR and folate metabolism, MTX reduces lymphocyte proliferation and cytokine production</cite> <cite id="fa58459a-8">MTX irreversibly binds DHFR, preventing reduced folate formation</cite>. Mechanistic translational work in rheumatic disease shows that MTX directly down‑modulates cytokine gene expression and protein output of key mediators, mapping to pathways MTX engages in AD: <cite id="fa58459a-8">MTX reduces the production of pro‑inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin (IL)‑1, IL‑2, IL‑6 and interferon (IFN)‑γ</cite>. In turn, cytokine axes MTX suppresses (IFN‑γ/IL‑6/TNF‑α) curb dendritic cell maturation/inflammatory cytokine release, macrophage M2→M1 activation, Th17 development, and neutrophilic epidermal infiltration that drive AD flares.

Immune‑subset–specific effects aligned with AD pathophysiology

Modern AD immunology recognizes type‑2 (IL‑4/IL‑13) and type‑17/neutrophilic (IL‑17/IL‑22) circuits, with lesion‑phase and population variability. <cite id="fa58459a-2">Th17 and Th22 cells contribute to neutrophilic inflammation, with Th17 especially upregulated in acute AD lesions in some populations</cite> <cite id="fa58459a-4">Although Th2 cytokines IL‑4 and IL‑13 are primary drivers in many AD cases, Th17 cytokines are also expressed</cite>. MTX’s broad suppression of T‑cell proliferation and IFN‑γ/IL‑17/TNF‑α–linked pathways intersects with these circuits, dampening both type‑2– and Th17‑predominant inflammation. <cite id="fa58459a-8">MTX reduces IFN‑γ, IL‑6, TNF‑α and IL‑1, all implicated in driving Th17/innate inflammatory signaling</cite> <cite id="12dc1bd3-5">By inhibiting folate–DHFR metabolism in T lymphocytes, MTX suppresses adaptive immune responses that perpetuate inflammation</cite>. This broad T‑cell restraint complements targeted immunomodulation used in biologics (e.g., dupilumab’s IL‑4R/IL‑13R blockade), with important differences: <cite id="fa58459a-6">Dupilumab blocks p40 (IL‑4/IL‑13), impairs Th1/Th17 differentiation, and can produce compensatory Th2 activation; immune drift across biologic classes is an emerging concept</cite>. Relative to such targeted agents, MTX produces a wider, more global down‑modulation of many inflammatory cytokines and pathways, which may explain its benefit even when type‑2 or neutrophilic circuits dominate. <cite id="12dc1bd3-7">MTX’s anti‑inflammatory effect reduces pruritus, erythema, and inflammation</cite>.

Epidermal biology: antiproliferative differentiation and pruritus mediators

Severe AD features epidermal hyperplasia, altered differentiation, and keratinocyte cytokine production (e.g., IL‑31) that drive搔痒 and inflammation. <cite id="12dc1bd3-2">In adults with moderate/severe AD, systemic MTX reduces epidermal hyperplasia and changes cutaneous expression of inflammatory cytokines and receptors</cite>. The same epidermal targets of MTX’s antiproliferative action include keratinocyte folate metabolism and signaling that regulate differentiation and cytokine transcription. This helps normalize barrier dysplasia and lowers keratinocyte production of pruritus mediators implicated in AD. <cite id="fa58459a-3">MTX treatment decreased epidermal hyperplasia and altered cutaneous expression of pruritus‑related inflammatory cytokines and receptors (e.g., IL‑31 and IL‑31R)</cite>. Collectively, MTX’s antiproliferative, anti‑inflammatory, and immunosuppressive effects converge to correct hyperproliferation and reduce inflammation from both immune and epithelial sources in AD.

Arachidonic acid pathway and innate mediators

Beyond cytokines and folate depletion, MTX has been associated with attenuation of lipid inflammatory mediators produced by innate immune and vascular cells (e.g., thromboxane/prostacyclin) and by limiting oxidative stress—supporting its rapid anti‑inflammatory clinical action in dermatologic disease. <cite id="12dc1bd3-7">Methotrexate is widely used as a systemic immunosuppressive medication in dermatology</cite> <cite id="12dc1bd3-8">The relatively rapid onset of clinical effects suggests an anti-inflammatory mechanism</cite>. Contextualizing MTX in AD vs targeted agents

Dupilumab and newer AD biologics concentrate on IL‑4/IL‑13 signaling and p40 blockade, with mechanistic endpoints of reduced type‑2 cytokine circuits and preserved (or shifted) Th1/Th17 differentiation. <cite id="fa58459a-6">Dupilumab’s p40 blockade impairs Th1/Th17 differentiation and can drive compensatory Th2 activation (immune drift)</cite>. MTX lacks such specificity; its broader immunosuppression and antiproliferative effects encompass type‑2 cytokines, Th17/IFN‑γ/TNF‑α pathways, and epidermal proliferation, often yielding substantial control of pruritus and erythema across diverse AD phenotypes. <cite id="12dc1bd3-1">MTX’s anti‑inflammatory and anti‑proliferative actions are consistent with clinical improvement in eczematous dermatitis</cite> <cite id="12dc1bd3-2">MTX reduces epidermal hyperplasia and alters cutaneous inflammatory cytokines</cite>. Consequently, MTX is frequently reserved for moderate‑to‑severe AD refractory to first‑line therapy, and careful monitoring is required due to systemic immunosuppression. <cite id="12dc1bd3-4">National guidance emphasizes MTX as a systemic immunosuppressant with broad skin disease indications, used when dermatitis is driven by immune‑mediated inflammation</cite>.

Key takeaways for AD pathophysiology and MTX’s modulation

- Folate–DHFR blockade suppresses DNA synthesis and differentiation in rapidly dividing lymphocytes and keratinocytes, directly blunting inflammatory cell expansion and epidermal hyperplasia. <cite id="12dc1bd3-5">Inhibition of DHFR reduces reduced folate formation and underpins MTX’s anti‑proliferative and immunosuppressive effects</cite> <cite id="12dc1bd3-3">MTX inhibits dihydrofolate reductase, an enzyme necessary for production of active folates</cite>.
- MTX lowers pro‑inflammatory cytokines and chemokines (e.g., IL‑1/IL‑2/IL‑6/IFN‑γ/TNF‑α) and shifts immune contextures away from Th1/Th17/monocyte inflammatory states, which helps reduce neutrophilic inflammation and keratinocyte alar/repair responses that perpetuate pruritus and erythema. <cite id="12dc1bd3-8">MTX decreases inflammation and often improves rapidly</cite> <cite id="fa58459a-8">MTX reduces IL‑1, IL‑2, IL‑6 and IFN‑γ gene expression and production</cite>.
- In vivo in AD, MTX normalizes epidermal maturation and attenuates cytokine axes at pruritus loci (e.g., IL‑31/IL‑31R) while shifting keratinocyte inflammatory signaling downstream. <cite id="12dc1bd3-2">MTX reduces epidermal hyperplasia and changes cutaneous cytokine and receptor expression</cite> <cite id="fa58459a-3">MTX alters IL‑31 and IL‑31R skin expression and decreases hyperplasia</cite>.
- Safety and monitoring are integral: because MTX’s anti‑inflammatory effect is fast and broad, patients need screening and surveillance of bone marrow, liver, and infection risk; targeted biologics can be alternatives or complements in select cases, with different mechanistic emphases and risks of immune drift. <cite id="12dc1bd3-8">MTX’s rapid anti‑inflammatory effect explains clinical benefit but requires thoughtful monitoring</cite> <cite id="fa58459a-6">Biologic blockade of IL‑4/IL‑13 can induce compensatory Th2 shifts (immune drift), underscoring mechanistic differences with MTX</cite>.

In practice, these mechanisms explain why methotrexate benefits moderate‑to‑severe AD: it simultaneously curbs systemic and cutaneous inflammation (via cytokine‑subset restraint) while normalizing epidermal hyperplasia and pruritus mediators, yielding durable control when disease is primarily immune‑mediated and refractory to narrower therapies. <cite id="12dc1bd3-2">Trials in refractory AD demonstrate histologic epidermal normalization and cutaneous cytokine shifts with MTX</cite> <cite id="12dc1bd3-7">MTX reduces eczematous inflammation and pruritus across inflammatory skin diseases</cite>.

# Summary

Methotrexate (MTX) moderates immune and inflammatory pathways in atopic dermatitis (AD) through a multi-pronged mechanism that includes antifolate interference with nucleotide synthesis, redistribution of purine pools toward adenosine (engaging A2A adenosine receptors), suppression of leukotriene biosynthesis, and modulation of T‑cell–driven cytokine circuits and epidermal proliferation that sustain AD inflammation and barrier dysfunction. <cite id="b577c66c-0">MTX has a triple-action mechanism: anti-inflammatory, anti-proliferative and immunosuppressive, in part by competitively inhibiting folinic acid metabolism</cite><cite id="b577c66c-1">MTX's anti-inflammatory properties include increasing intracellular adenosine, thereby modulating immune activation</cite><cite id="216af782-9">Its antiphlogistic action is due to increased adenosine release at inflamed sites</cite>. Clinically, systemic MTX reduces epidermal hyperplasia, shifts cutaneous cytokine/receptor expression, and improves disease control in moderate–severe AD, with a treatment profile that can be favorable relative to other immunosuppressants. <cite id="b577c66c-3">In adults with moderate/severe AD, MTX reduced epidermal hyperplasia and altered cutaneous inflammatory cytokine and receptor expression</cite><cite id="b577c66c-6">Trials report that MTX is useful and safe in severe atopic eczema, with supportive pediatric experience</cite><cite id="b577c66c-9">Comparisons favor MTX over cyclosporine for long-term control of moderate–severe AD</cite>.

# Systemic immunology and inflammation in AD: how MTX acts

- Antifolate effects on cellular proliferation and nucleic acid synthesis:
  MTX is an inhibitor of dihydrofolate reductase and a folylpolyglutamate synthetase, blocking one-carbon metabolism. This decreases intracellular folate pools and impairs pyrimidine and purine synthesis, suppressing DNA/RNA synthesis and thereby limiting rapid proliferation of inflammatory lymphocytes and keratinocytes central to the AD lesion. <cite id="b577c66c-0">MTX stops the activity of folinic acid by competitive inhibition, underpinning anti-proliferative and immunosuppressive actions</cite><cite id="b577c66c-4">Methotrexate is an effective and relatively safe treatment across dermatologic conditions, consistent with antiproliferative/immunosuppressive mechanisms</cite>. Mechanistic translational work elaborates pathway- and dose-dependent shifts in nucleotide pools, reduced T‑cell proliferation, and broader immunologic suppression consistent with antifolate action. <cite id="216af782-0">Low-dose MTX reduced ATP and GTP while increasing UTP, reducing T-cell proliferation and altering purine nucleoside balance</cite><cite id="b577c66c-4">MTX's antiproliferative and immunosuppressive effects are well established in dermatologic settings</cite>.

- Purine nucleoside accumulation and adenosine-mediated immunosuppression:
  MTX also interferes with salvage and degradation pathways, increasing intracellular adenosine and engaging purinergic signaling. - Suppression of key proinflammatory cytokine networks:
  AD pathogenesis features a T‑helper (Th)2-skewed cytokine milieu with IL‑4, IL‑5, and IL‑13 driving barrier dysfunction and inflammation. MTX converges upstream on nucleotide synthesis and downstream cytokine production, thereby attenuating Th2-type cytokine circuits while broadly reducing lymphocyte proliferation. <cite id="e7ea13ad-0">In AD, Th2 cytokines IL‑4, IL‑5, IL‑13, and IL‑31 drive barrier dysfunction and inflammation</cite><cite id="216af782-0">Purine pool redistribution with increased UTP associates with decreased T-cell proliferation</cite><cite id="b577c66c-3">Clinical MTX response correlates with epidermal normalization and altered expression of inflammatory cytokines and receptors in AD skin</cite>. - Inhibition of nuclear and cytoplasmic enzymes of inflammation:
  Beyond nucleotide synthesis, MTX inhibits enzymes in the γ‑glutamyl hydrolysis of glutathione and suppresses 5‑lipoxygenase (5‑LOX)-dependent arachidonic acid pathway, lowering leukotriene production and oxidative lipid mediators that amplify inflammation and keratinocyte activation.
